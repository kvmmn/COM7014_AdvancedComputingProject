2. Terminology & Conceptual Foundation

To ensure precision and transparency across all documentation, the central technical terms are defined below:

Term Working Definition Role in This Research
Time-Series Foundation Model (TSFM) A large pre-trained neural network (predominantly Transformer architectures) trained on massive multi-domain time-series corpora, designed to produce zero-shot forecasts without local retraining. The advanced deep learning challenger under evaluation (e.g. Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5, MOIRAI).
GIFT-Eval General Time Series Foundation Model Evaluation. A standardized benchmark released by Salesforce AI Research (Aksu et al., 2024) comprising 23 datasets, 144k series, and 177M observations across 7 domains. Serves as our primary benchmark layer, offering established baselines and documented data leakage tracking.
Zero-Shot Evaluation Testing a model on time-series distributions completely unseen during pretraining, without updating network weights. The empirical evaluation setting used to test true cross-domain generalisation.
Test-Data Leakage When an evaluation dataset was inadvertently present in a model's pretraining corpus, producing artificially inflated zero-shot scores. Audited explicitly; dictates which foundation model checkpoints are methodologically valid for this study.
Intermittent Demand Series characterised by frequent zero observations punctuated by irregular non-zero values (e.g. spare parts, retail inventory, medical emergencies). A documented weakness of neural foundation models where classical statistical estimators frequently prevail.
Croston / SBA / TSB Classical parametric methods designed specifically for intermittent series. Croston separates interval from magnitude; SBA corrects positive bias; TSB updates demand probabilities continuously. Specialised lightweight CPU baselines for sparse data subsets.
Catch22 Features Canonical Time-Series Characteristics. A scientifically curated set of 22 computationally efficient summary statistics capturing autocorrelation, distribution shape, outliers, and entropy in <5ms. The lightweight input features examined by the routing classifier.
Calibration & ECE Expected Calibration Error. The statistical alignment between a model's predicted confidence score and its actual empirical accuracy. The primary metric ensuring the router outputs trustworthy probabilities rather than uncalibrated decisions.

3. The Core Research Problem

Recent industry practice often defaults to deploying large Time-Series Foundation Models indiscriminately. However, empirical literature highlights two critical operational realities:

1. Computational Expense & Latency
Foundation models require dedicated GPU hardware and incur significant inference latency. Running zero-shot transformers across millions of enterprise series generates high recurring infrastructure costs.
2. Degradation on Sparse Series
Cross-benchmark meta-analyses (e.g. Rubczynski, 2026 across 543 comparisons) demonstrate that foundation models regularly underperform classical statistical methods (Croston, SBA) and GBDTs on intermittent data.
The Central Objective: Rather than viewing foundation models as a universal replacement, this research builds a fast, calibrated per-series router that evaluates individual time-series properties and determines whether to dispatch to a foundation model or fall back to an efficient classical baseline.

4. Dataset Discovery & Structural Taxonomy

Following supervisory advice, a 6-route discovery protocol was executed across multiple academic repositories, logging 29 runs and achieving multi-domain representation across 8 industries.

Structural Anatomy of Time-Series Data

To ensure the router can generalise, candidate datasets were analysed across their structural and temporal properties:

  • Dimensionality: Univariate single-target series ($y_t$) versus multivariate coupled channels.
  • Sampling Frequency: Ranging from high-frequency (15-minute grid telemetry) to daily (retail) and monthly (industrial parts).
  • Exogenous Variables: Dynamic covariates (temperatures, promotions, calendar events) and static category tags.

Intermittency Classification: The Syntetos-Boylan Quadrant

To objectively identify where classical models should supersede foundation models, candidate series are categorized using two mathematical dimensions: average inter-demand interval ($p$) and squared coefficient of variation ($CV^2$):

Data Contamination Audit & Clean Model Triad

An inspection of pretraining logs revealed that older models (Chronos-T5, TimesFM 1.0, TinyTimeMixer) ingested Monash and M-competition datasets during pretraining. Evaluating these models on those benchmarks would violate zero-shot evaluation standards.

To preserve scientific validity, this study isolates the leakage-clean triad:

  • Chronos-2: Employs strict, verified partition boundaries.
  • TimesFM-2.5: Cleaned, segregated evaluation splits.
  • MOIRAI: Pretrained on LOTSA with documented masking of benchmark targets.

5. Model Landscape & Proposed Routing Pipeline

The router evaluates candidate time series using 22 computationally fast statistical features (Catch22) computed in under 5 ms, predicting model suitability and calibration scores before executing a forecast: 

sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    actor System as Ingestion Pipeline
    participant Extractor as Feature Extractor (Catch22)
    participant Router as Calibrated Router (LightGBM)
    participant Gate as Decision Gate
    participant Forecaster as Model Execution

    System->>Extractor: Pass Series Y[t]
    Extractor->>Extractor: Compute 22 summary features in <5ms
(Autocorrelation, Sparsity, Variance, Entropy)
    Extractor->>Router: Feature Vector x
    Router->>Router: Predict probability P(FM wins | x)
    Router->>Gate: Calibrated Confidence P + Estimated Cost
    alt High Confidence & Budget Available
        Gate->>Forecaster: Execute Foundation Model (Chronos-2 / TimesFM-2.5)
    else High Sparsity Detected
        Gate->>Forecaster: Execute Intermittent Specialist (Croston / SBA)
    else Low Confidence or Standard Seasonality
        Gate->>Forecaster: Execute Classical Baseline (AutoARIMA / ETS)
    end
    Forecaster-->>System: Return Final Forecast

6. Candidate Proposal Titles for Review

In accordance with supervisory guidance to review the title following Phase M1 findings, five options are presented below for consideration:

Option 1: The Reliability & Calibration Focus Student Preference
"When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: A Calibrated Reliability Framework for Cross-Family Forecasting Routing"
Rationale: Directly addresses the "when to trust" problem while highlighting the primary technical novelty: outputting calibrated confidence scores on model trustworthiness across diverse model families.
Option 2: The Practical Computational Cost & Efficiency Focus
"Cost-Calibrated Per-Series Routing: Balancing Foundation Models and Classical Baselines in Operational Time-Series Forecasting"
Rationale: Focuses on the industrial trade-off: eliminating unnecessary GPU overhead when lightweight statistical models deliver equivalent or superior accuracy.
Option 3: The Intermittent Demand & Edge-Case Focus
"Selective Trust in Time-Series Forecasting: Routing Edge Cases and Intermittent Series Between Foundation Models and Classical Specialists"
Rationale: Emphasizes the empirical finding that foundation models falter on sparse data, framing the router as an intelligent edge-case detector.
Option 4: The Systems & Dispatcher Architecture Focus
"A Risk-Aware Per-Series Dispatcher: Calibrated Routing Across Classical and Pretrained Time-Series Foundation Models"
Rationale: Uses systems engineering terminology ("dispatcher"), presenting the artefact as a modular computing component.
Option 5: Current Working Baseline
"When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: A Per-Series Reliability Framework for Routing Between Foundation Models and Classical Forecasting Models"
Rationale: The existing title currently registered in initial administrative drafts.
