01Summary

M1 had to settle two things: the datasets and the title.

The datasets were chosen by a systematic search across fields, first in benchmark registries and then in the papers that use the data. The search did more than produce a list. It showed that routing on the obvious retail datasets is already published, which changed both the corpus and the title.

Recommended title

When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: Calibrated Per-Series Routing Between Foundation Models and Classical Forecasters

What the search changed: per-series routing between foundation models and classical forecasters already exists, on M5 and Favorita. The open part is whether a router's confidence can be trusted, including on a field it never saw. So the corpus needs a held-out field, and the title needs to name calibration.

Terms used on this page
Foundation model (FM)A pretrained time-series model used without further training (zero-shot), such as Chronos-2.
Classical fallbackSeasonal Naive, ETS, ARIMA and Theta, plus Croston, SBA and TSB on demand series.
RouterA small model that decides, per series, whether to trust the FM, and how confident it is.
CalibratedDecisions made at 80% confidence are right about 80% of the time.
FieldAn application area such as retail, energy or banking. A held-out field is never seen in router training.
OverlapA test series that was in an FM's pretraining data, which would inflate its zero-shot score.
02Problem

Why the choice of datasets decides the result.

Foundation models win on benchmark averages and lose on particular series. A router that decides when to trust them can only learn from, and be tested on, the variety in its data. Data from one field would make it look better or worse than it is.

Broad benchmark144K series

GIFT-Eval spans seven fields and ten frequencies; FMs are among its strongest entries.

03Search

A protocol first, then two passes of search.

Before searching, the selection rules were written down: which fields to cover, what makes a dataset usable, and when to stop. Pass 1 searched registries, repositories and field archives. Pass 2 went to the literature and extracted, paper by paper, which datasets model-selection and routing studies actually use.

04Papers

The papers showed which datasets are already taken.

Reading how datasets are used turned up three studies that already route individual series between model families on M5 and Favorita. Those datasets stay useful for comparison, but they cannot carry a claim of novelty. What none of the studies reports is a checked confidence, or a test on a field the router never saw.

Studies that shaped the dataset choice

FAME2026 · preprintClosest overlap

Sparse expert routing on SNBC, M5 and Favorita; calls Chronos for 8.6% and 10.9% of series.

M5 and Favorita allow direct comparison, but cannot show originality.

Zhang2025Direct precedent

24,646 Favorita item–store series; 57.2% mean zero sales; 34 features; 14- and 30-day horizons.

Its Favorita protocol is the comparison point for cross-family routing.

CARFS2023Transfer precedent

Model shortlist learned on M5, tested on 1,000 Favorita series.

Retail-to-retail transfer is done; a stronger test leaves out a whole field.

Werling et al.2023Held-out field

Ausgrid: 300 homes over three years; selector trained on 200, evaluated on 100.

An energy dataset with a ready-made split for testing on unseen entities.

Barak et al.2019Compact demand

NN5: 111 daily ATM series, 791 observations each, 56-day horizon.

Non-retail demand, usable if missing observations can be separated from true zeros.

FFORMPP2022Method anchor

M1 and M3 plus 10,000 GRATIS series per frequency; evaluated on M4.

Build and evaluate the router on separate data; synthetic series only as support.

AutoForecastBreadth source

348 datasets, 625 series and 322 model configurations across fields.

A candidate source only after per-source provenance and licence checks.

06Title

The title follows from what the search found.

The registered title can be refined after M1. The search gave four tests for a better one: keep the question “when to trust”, name what is actually new (checked confidence), avoid implying that routing itself is new, and stay true to all three dataset layers rather than one.

RegisteredCurrent

When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: A Per-Series Reliability Framework for Routing Between Foundation Models and Classical Forecasting Models

Keeps the question, but “reliability framework for routing” does not say confidence will be checked.

RecommendedProposed after M1

When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: Calibrated Per-Series Routing Between Foundation Models and Classical Forecasters

Keeps the question and names calibration as the contribution; fits every dataset layer.

AlternativeConsidered

When to Trust a Time-Series Foundation Model: A Calibrated Reliability Framework for Cross-Family Forecasting Routing

Names calibration, but puts weight on cross-family routing, which the papers show is not new.

AlternativeConsidered

Leads with cost, which is one scorecard rather than the main question.

AlternativeConsidered

Fits the sparse-demand layer, but narrows the scope below the corpus.

AlternativeConsidered

A Risk-Aware Per-Series Dispatcher: Calibrated Routing Across Classical and Pretrained Time-Series Foundation Models

Frames the work as a system build rather than a research question.

07Tests

What the corpus will be used to test.

Main research question

Can a per-series router give a calibrated probability that trusting a time-series foundation model is the right choice, and does that probability still hold on datasets and fields it was not trained on?

RQ1
Signals · Which series characteristics predict that the FM will win?

Fails if feature-based prediction does no better than always predicting the majority outcome on held-out datasets.

RQ2
Value · Can routing match or beat the best single model at lower cost?

Fails if always-FM or always-classical matches or beats the router on error at equal or lower measured cost.

RQ3 ★
Trust · Is the trust probability calibrated, including on the held-out field?

Fails if held-out calibration error exceeds a pre-declared bound, or error does not fall as coverage is reduced.

RQ4
Sparse demand · Can the router spot intermittent series in advance and send them to the fallback?

Fails if it does no better than the fixed Syntetos–Boylan rule, on demand series only.

How each layer is used

What “checked confidence” means

Calibration

Stated confidence should match how often the choice is right.

Deferral

Trusting the FM on fewer series should mean fewer errors.

Transfer

The same check must hold on a field the router never saw.

Schematics explain the tests. They are not results.

08Decisions

Decisions to close M1.

  1. CorpusGIFT-Eval for breadth; M5, Favorita, Car Parts and NN5 for comparison; Ausgrid as the held-out field; the listed reserves if a gate fails.
  2. TitleAdopt the recommended title after M1, or keep the registered one.
  3. Common groundA univariate core comparison with scaled errors and each dataset's published horizon; covariates outside the core.
  4. ContributionCalibrated trust-or-defer, tested for transfer, as the distinction from FAME, Zhang, CARFS, TimeRouter and TW3Cast.
  5. ValidationLeave-one-dataset-out testing, energy-free router training for the Ausgrid test, and uncertain overlap pairs excluded from headline results.
  6. Scope and rightsLightGBM as a required reference baseline outside the routing pool; model licences, Kaggle terms and upstream conditions of each dataset confirmed.
Current boundary. No dataset has been downloaded, no model has been run, and no experimental result is claimed. Next: licence, provenance, overlap and feasibility checks on the six shortlisted datasets, then proposal and ethics approval.
