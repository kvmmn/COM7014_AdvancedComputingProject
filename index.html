3. Dataset Structural Taxonomy & Mathematical Characterization

As advised by Dr. Pattanaik, we examined the physical schemas, sampling rates, and statistical characteristics of candidate data to ensure the router has meaningful patterns to learn from and evaluate.

3.1 Schema Taxonomy Across Time-Series Data

In modern enterprise environments, time-series data present four structural dimensions:

  • Time Resolution: Uniform fixed intervals (10-second web telemetry, 15-minute grid load, daily sales, monthly inventory).
  • Channel Dimensionality: Univariate series ($y_t \in \mathbb{R}$) versus multivariate coupled vectors ($\mathbf{y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$) with cross-channel correlation.
  • Dynamic Covariates ($X_t$): Time-varying external signals such as ambient temperature (energy), promotional discounts (retail), or holiday calendars.
  • Static Metadata ($Z$): Fixed contextual attributes such as store identifier, geographic region, or asset class.

3.2 Intermittency Classification: The Syntetos-Boylan Matrix

To determine objectively which model family is mathematically suited for each series, we apply the Syntetos-Boylan classification using average inter-demand interval ($p$) and squared coefficient of variation ($CV^2$):

Syntetos-Boylan Empirical Demand Quadrant
Cutoff thresholds: Inter-demand interval $p = 1.32$ periods · Squared coefficient of variation $CV^2 = 0.49$
ERRATIC DEMAND High Variance Dense
p ≤ 1.32  |  CV² > 0.49
Frequent demand but highly fluctuating transaction magnitudes. Non-zero values vary widely.
Optimal Model Family: GBDT (LightGBM) / Hybrid ARIMA
LUMPY DEMAND Sparse & Volatile
p > 1.32  |  CV² > 0.49
Long runs of zero demand combined with large, volatile bursts. Highest forecasting difficulty.
Optimal Model Family: TSB / Croston Variant
SMOOTH DEMAND Continuous & Regular
p ≤ 1.32  |  CV² ≤ 0.49
Regular occurrences, stable non-zero volumes. Consistent trend and seasonal patterns.
Optimal Model Family: Foundation Models (TSFM)
INTERMITTENT DEMAND Classic Sparse
p > 1.32  |  CV² ≤ 0.49
Infrequent occurrences (many zeros) but relatively stable order sizes when non-zero.
Optimal Model Family: Croston / SBA (Classical)

4. Model Architecture & Selection Analysis

A core research contribution is establishing a principled basis for model inclusion. We specifically addressed why certain models are selected while dozens of other published architectures were deliberately excluded.

4.1 The Pretraining Data Contamination Audit

In foundational machine learning, evaluating a model on series present in its pretraining corpus invalidates zero-shot claims. Our inspection of model documentation revealed extensive training contamination:

4.2 Methodological Guidance: Where Do LSTMs, CNN-LSTMs, and Hybrids Fit?

An important question arises regarding locally trained neural networks (e.g. LSTMs, CNN-LSTM hybrids, or the Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM models used in prior industrial experience at Snappstore): Should they be considered classical, and should they be included in the routing pool?

Paradigm 1: Classical Statistical
AutoARIMA, ETS, Croston, SBA: Fitted per series in milliseconds on CPU. Zero training pipelines. Highly interpretable, low memory footprint.
Paradigm 2: Local Deep Learning
LSTM, CNN-LSTM, Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM: Requires local backpropagation training per series or per dataset. Captures non-linear residuals well, but requires extensive training pipelines.
Paradigm 3: Zero-Shot Foundation
Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5: Pre-trained on millions of series. Zero training at test time. Immediate inference, but GPU-demanding.
Methodological Recommendation for This Project:

1. The Primary Routing Boundary Remains Cross-Family: The router’s core operational value is deciding between Zero-Shot Foundation Models (heavy GPU inference, zero local training) and Lightweight Classical Baselines (instant CPU inference, Croston/AutoARIMA).

2. In Corporate Deployments (e.g. Snappstore): A Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM is highly effective when forecasting a small number of high-value aggregated series where offline retraining pipelines are manageable. However, across a catalogue of 100,000 retail SKUs, training 100,000 individual LSTMs is computationally prohibitive — which is precisely why companies explore zero-shot foundation models.

3. Incorporating Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM as a Benchmark Comparator: We propose including Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM and a standard LSTM / DeepAR as a representative locally trained deep learning baseline in our empirical evaluation table in Phase M2. This connects real-world industrial practice directly to academic evaluation without complicating the lightweight per-series router.
