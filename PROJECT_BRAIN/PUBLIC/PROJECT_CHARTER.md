# Public Project Charter

## Purpose

Develop a substantial Master's-level computing project that addresses a defensible organisational or computing problem, produces an appropriate artefact, and evaluates both the artefact and the project process with evidence.

## Current scope

The working topic is selected. See [`TOPIC.md`](../../TOPIC.md).

The project will design and evaluate a per-series reliability router that chooses between a time-series foundation model and a classical forecaster, and that attaches a calibrated confidence to the choice. Dataset choice, the exact model pool, supervisor approval, and the formal proposal remain open. Data collection and artefact development have not started.

## Quality principles

- Brief and rubric first.
- Evidence before narrative.
- One clear aim and around five to seven challenging but achievable objectives.
- Requirements must be actionable, measurable, testable, complete, clear, and unambiguous.
- Accessibility, security, ethics, sustainability, social responsibility, and global context are design concerns from the beginning.
- An artefact alone is not enough. It must be contextualised, tested, validated, compared, and critically evaluated.
- Public outputs must be reproducible, lawful to share, and free of secrets or personal data.

## Checkpoints

1. Complete authority pack.
2. Select and justify topic.
3. Confirm working title, aim, rationale, objectives, deliverables, word budget, and evaluation baseline.
4. Register on ARMS and obtain the required approvals.
5. Design and approve requirements, methodology, data plan, test plan, and timeline.
6. Implement with continuous evidence capture.
7. Verify, validate, evaluate, and freeze results.
8. Draft section by section after student confirmation.
9. Run technical, academic, publication, and visual quality gates before submission or release.
